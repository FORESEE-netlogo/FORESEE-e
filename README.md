The FORESEE model was developed to simulate earthworm populations in the field and to predict possible effects on the populations due to exposure to plant protection products. 
The model can simulate separate populations of the three ecological earthworm types epigeic, endogeic, or anecic, which describe the movement of the worm in the soil or at the surface: 
•	Epigeic worms, here represented by Eisenia fetida, move solely on the surface, 
•	Endogeic worms, here represented by Aporrectodea caliginosa, move only in the soil in non-permanent burrows, and 
•	Anecic worms, here represented by Lumbricus terrestris, stay in a permanent burrow during daytime and forage on the surface at night. 
Their movement is driven by environmental factors, such as food (represented by organic matter content), soil moisture, soil temperature, and soil bulk density. These parameters must therefore be provided with spatial and, for moisture and temperature, temporal resolution to run simulations with the model.
